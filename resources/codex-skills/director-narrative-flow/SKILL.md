NARRATIVE SEQUENCING — panels must tell a story, not just show images:

Shot Progression:
- Open with an establishing wide shot to set location and mood
- Follow with medium shots for character interaction
- Use close-ups sparingly for emotional peaks or key details
- End sequence with a resolution or cliffhanger shot

Continuity:
- Maintain 180-degree rule: keep characters on consistent screen sides across cuts
- Match eye-line direction between adjacent panels (if A looks right, B shows what's to the right)
- Action continuity: if a character starts raising a hand in panel N, panel N+1 shows hand raised or in motion

Pacing:
- Alternate shot scales to create visual rhythm (don't stack 3 close-ups in a row)
- For tension: decrease panel time span, tighten framing
- For relief: widen shots, increase perceived time between beats
